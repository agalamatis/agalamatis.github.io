Timeline of the last Árpád kings and the Angevin claims

  • 1270-08-06: Double wedding of the heirs of Anjou and Árpád: Prince Charles II of Naples (aged 16) with Princess Maria (aged 15), and her brother, Prince László IV the Cuman (aged 8), with Elisabetta d'Anjou (aged 9). The marriages were meant as a double alliance between the kingdoms to support the intended conquest of Byzantium, which never materialised.
  • 1272-08-06: King István V of Hungary dies and is succeeded by his eldest son, László IV the Cuman.
  • 1274-03-07: Death of Tommaso d'Aquino.
  • 1274-05-18 to 1274-07-17: The Second Council of Lyon decides on the union of the Churches and discusses, inconclusively, plans for more crusades [wikipedia].
  • 1276: King Uroš I of Serbia is deposed by his eldest son, Dragutin.
  • 1278: András III the Venetian arrives in Hungary at the invitation of a rebellious baron, Iván Kőszegi, who tries to play him off against László IV; the conspiracy collapses and András returns to Venice.
  • 1278-08-26: László IV allies himself with the Habsburgs against the Přemyslids, and his forces play a pre-eminent role in the Battle on the Marchfeld [wikipedia].
  • 1278-09 to 1278-12: The papal legate, Filippo di Fermo, comes to help László IV consolidate his authority, but is shocked at the presence of thousands of pagan Cumans. László promises to force them to adopt a Christian way of life, but they refuse the legate's demands; László sides with the Cumans, and the legate excommunicates him. The Cumans imprison the legate, and the legate's partisans capture László.
  • 1280-01 to 1280-03: László IV agrees to persuade the Cumans to submit to Filippo di Fermo.
  • 1282-03-30 to 1282-04-28: Rebellion of the Sicilian Vespers [wikipedia].
  • 1282: King Dragutin of Serbia abdicates in favour of his younger brother, Milutin.
  • 1283: Marriage of King Milutin of Serbia and Princess Erzsebet, sister of László IV.
  • 1284: Princess Erzsebet dies, King Milutin marries Princess Ana the Bulgar, and László IV makes Dragutin Ban of Syrmia, beginning a low-level civil war in the kingdom.
  • 1284: The Sicilian admiral Roger of Lauria captures Charles II of Naples in a naval battle near Naples.
  • 1285-01-07: King Charles I of Naples dies while his only son is imprisoned in Aragon; the kingdom is ruled by regents for his grandson, Charles Martel.
  • 1285 to 1286: The Second Mongol Invasion of Hungary [wikipedia].
  • 1286-09: László IV imprisons his wife, Elisabetta d'Anjou, on Margaret Island, to live with his Cuman mistresses, at the suggestion of his sister Erzsebet.
  • 1288: Charles Robert is born.
  • 1289: Queen Maria of Naples, with the help of the King of France and the Pope, negotiates the release of her husband, King Charles II of Naples, so that he can be formally crowned.
  • 1289: Queen Maria frees Elisabetta d'Anjou and takes her under her protection back to her brother, King Charles II of Naples.
  • 1290: Queen Maria announces her claim to the Crown of Saint Stephen, stating that the male line of the House of Árpád became extinct with the death of her brother, László IV of Hungary and Croatia. However, her father's cousin, András III the Venetian, also lays claim to the throne through his father, István the Posthumous, even though all the other members of the royal family regarded him as a bastard.
  • 1290-07-23: Despite Queen Maria's claims, the Hungarian lords and prelates prefer András, and he is crowned king.
  • 1292-01: With the encouragement of the Croatian nobility (the Babonići, Frankopani and Šubići), Queen Maria transfers her claim to her son, Charles Martel.
  • 1294: Queen Fenenna, first wife of András III, confirms on Elisabetta d'Anjou the privilege of collecting the donations of the Church in Veszprém County.
  • 1295-08-12: Charles Martel dies, and his mother's claim passes to her grandson, Charles Robert.
  • 1296-01-15: Marriage of King András III of Hungary and Agnes of Austria, daughter of King Albrecht I of Germany and first cousin of Charles Robert.
  • 1296-02: King Charles II of Naples successfully petitions Pope Bonifacius VIII to exclude Charles Martel's line from the succession of Naples in favour of his living son, Robert. Contemporary scholars of law (Durante and Villani) condemn the decision, while much later, when the question of the Naples succession resurfaces, Baldus refrains from condemning it.
  • 1299: King András III appoints his maternal uncle, Albertino Morosini, Ban of Slavonia and heir after his future sons, since he is childless. Albertino sets himself up in Venetian-allied Ragusa, preparing to pacify the disloyal Croatian lords and styling himself (co-)King of Croatia.
  • 1300-01: This drives the Croatian prelates from resistance to open rebellion, and so Juraj Šubić of Trogir travels to Naples on behalf of his brother, Pavao I Šubić of Bribir, to ask Charles Robert to claim the crown in person. King Charles II accepts and borrows 1,300 ounces of gold from Florentine bankers to finance his grandson's journey.

Prequel: The Siege of Acre (1291)

By 1291 the Kingdom of Jerusalem had shrunk to a strip of coast around Acre, its capital, the seat of the Latin Patriarch and the headquarters of the Templars, the Hospitallers and the Teutonic Order, with the fortified quarters of Venice, Genoa and Pisa. In August 1290 newly arrived Italian crusaders massacred Muslim merchants and peasants around the city, and Sultan Qalawun of Egypt took it as his cause for war. He died on the march that November, but his son, al-Ashraf Khalil, refused every offer of peace.

In January 1291 the Grand Master of the Teutonic Order, Burchard von Schwanden, resigned and left Acre for Europe. In the spring the Genoese made their own peace and withdrew, and many women and children were sent to Cyprus. On 6 April the sultan's army camped before the walls, and the great trebuchets of the Syrian contingents arrived two days later. For six weeks the defenders raided the Mamluk lines, among them the Templars' night attack with Greek fire on 27 April, while the mangonels battered the walls and the sappers mined the towers. King Henry II came from Cyprus on 4 May with some 700 men, but judged the city lost, and the last parley ended when a stone from the walls landed near the sultan's tent.

Before dawn on 18 May the whole Mamluk army attacked to the sound of drums. By nine in the morning the Accursed Tower had fallen and the Grand Master of the Temple, Guillaume de Beaujeu, was mortally wounded at the Gate of Saint Anthony. By the afternoon the Mamluks were in the streets, and the flight to the harbour became a scramble for the few ships left, their captains selling passage for fortunes. The Templar compound held out until 28 May, when its last tower, undermined, surrendered and collapsed.

Pavo, a fisherman's son of the Zadar islands given to the Teutonic Order as a boy, was a young brother-sergeant in Acre. He survived its fall, bearing the shield of the last Teuton brother to die on its walls, bathed in their blood, and he has carried it ever since; it is why he is called Pavo d'Acri.

1st Story: Proserpina

In February 1300 the group crossed the Adriatic aboard the Proserpina, a sailboat hired under Captain Garvyn, escorting Queen Mária of Naples and the dowager queen Elisabetta on a secret mission: to negotiate with Mária's sisters, Katalina and Erzsebet, in Serbia, and so secure the claim of her grandson, Charles Robert, to the Crown of Saint Stephen. With them sailed the queen's confessor, Father Mateo, and Salvatore.

Captain Garvyn turned out to be haunted by the ghosts of bodies he had thrown into the sea. While the group was confronting the spectres the crew revolted, and the damaged ship was wrecked on the southern shores of the island of Vis on Sunday 29 February 1300. There they met Casimir, a Serbian ranger stranded on the island, who joined them.

Vis is heavily forested and ringed by steep cliffs. A cave system on the southern shore, reached by climbing or by a small rowboat, led to an alchemical or necromantic laboratory connected to the mausoleum of the Morosini family, who rule the island; the wrecked Proserpina was abandoned there. Two paths circle the island's central peak, and midway along them stand two "black marble" statues, each about three metres tall, of cloaked and hooded women hiding their weeping faces with one hand and pointing west with the other.

The Morosini live in a massive mansion with its own seaport on the hill west of the village, and trade with the other Venetian islands and the mainland. They seemed of lesser intelligence, their blood was clotted, and they did not seem to feel pain, even when mutilated. Between the mansion and the church stood a small abandoned Byzantine church, most of its Orthodox saints defaced and its iconostasis painted with satanic images. The small village on the northern shore, fewer than a hundred souls living from fishing and farming, helped the group against the nobles ruling the island, though reluctantly.

The group stole the Morosini sailboat, and aboard it found a mysterious letter in which "Christ", the "sheep-lion" and the "Creator" carried double meanings, and the Apocalypse was implied. In it they sailed on for the Serbian coast.

Side-story: The Rozafa Castle Haunting

On Friday 5 March 1300 the group landed at Ulcinj and passed themselves off as pilgrims bound for the monastery of Ostrog, a day north-west of Podgorica; the queens gave them 200 silver giglia for the journey, 120 of which bought six horses. In the harbour lay a huge vessel flying the flag of the Byzantine emperor, Andronikos II: his embassy, led by Theodore Metochites, had brought his six-year-old daughter, Simonis, to marry King Milutin, and the wedding and the peace treaty were to be negotiated at the monastery of Jelena in the fortress of Shkodër. The Venetian merchants said that Erzsebet had died years before, impious and having given the king only daughters, and that his queen was now Ana, daughter of the Bulgarian tsar, to be set aside for Simonis.

Meanwhile Captain Garvyn left the boat, got drunk in the tavern and told everyone that he was carrying two Saxon princesses; the players had to stop Queen Mária from murdering him. Metochites took Leonardo up the hill to the underground shop of a sketchy hunchback calling himself Yuri, full of satanic and Arabic writings, who gave him twenty doses of "liquid fire" and ancient texts whose contents he would not disclose.

On Saturday 6 March the group set out for Shkodër with the Byzantines: the imperial carriage with Simonis and her handmaiden, Zoe, and six Varangian guards, while Casimir and Salvatore scouted ahead. That morning the two confessed their dreams to each other in secret, then to Leonardo and to Mateo. Casimir had dreamed of a black-cloaked figure, like the statues of Vis, in a basalt gothic cathedral, pointing to an open book whose illumination showed a golden-skinned Virgin and an uncanny child, and three Magi bringing a rotting corpse, a chest of mutilated remains and the same book, repeating without end. Salvatore had dreamed of a young girl chased across the fields by black-clad Orthodox priests, washed and fed with blood and walled up alive, while she encouraged them and her family screamed. Mateo read Casimir's dream as a perversion of the Nativity, the Magi bringing gifts to the Antichrist born of the Whore of Babylon, and when Casimir told the queens, he began to think that Mária looked like the Virgin of his dream.

At Rozafa, the fortress of Shkodër, an old nun holding a torch welcomed them: Sister Brnjača, Jelena's daughter, who told Pavo that the castle was sixty years old, built on older ruins. Giovanni sent Salvatore and Casimir to look for the place from the dream, and on the walls they saw the ghostly girl embracing a battlement, sinking into the stones and whispering "please give me some water"; the water Salvatore poured pooled like blood and was sucked into the walls in veins. Jelena received the two queens, called them her sisters, said she was happy to see them in her last moments, and claimed to be a child of Charles of Anjou, born before Charles II. Mária said that the black figure of Casimir's dream recalled a legend of her father's Árpád side: a form like the Holy Ghost that makes men write stories about the family's enemies in the castle of Buda. A stone fell from the new walls onto another ghostly figure, and Pavo, climbing the walls, saw veins of blood climbing over them and a figure staring at him, begging for water as it sank into the ground. Brnjača admitted that she had been investigating the ghosts, which have haunted the castle for generations.

The next morning Mária rode for Trgovište, leaving Elisabetta in charge in Shkodër: to mend relations with Brnjača, to keep an eye on the negotiations between the Serbs and the Byzantines, to end "the Garvyn loose end", and to investigate the haunting of Rozafa. Salvatore "executed" Garvyn, who lies buried near the basilica. Presbyter Vukašin, versed in both the eastern and western rites but suspected of manipulation, shared a few confessional "asides": Barjan and Flaka had seen the girl ghost on the walls, and the former presbyter had killed himself because he had committed a deadly sin, according to the records of the patriarchate of Peć.

In the town the group met Dragana, the Slav keeper of the Pelican's Nest tavern, and her husband Barjan, an Albanian cook of the old local families, whose brother Florin had disappeared in the castle in their youth, before Jelena and the Orthodox clergy came. They bought cloth from Mimoza, a spinner whose Italian was poor, and her friend Vera, the healer's apprentice, translated for her and took them to her grandmother Flaka, the hard-drinking matriarch of the Tophane cotton farms. Flaka told how Barjan and Florin had explored the haunted castle to find Rozafa's hair so that Florin could marry her, and how "the former presbyter married Florin with Rozafa in Hell". The group also puzzled over a rope-and-noose mystery in the underwall pass of the north wall, and over tombstones from before the first Mongol invasion bearing only Slavic first names, such as TATIANA, 1132–1207, and LUKA, 1198–1238.

On Wednesday 10 March Metochites and Giovanni left for the patriarchate of Ohrid. On Thursday 11 March Leonardo and his companions resolved the Rozafa and Vukašin affair, to Brnjača's satisfaction, and Leonardo came away from Shkodër haunted by the soul of Rozafa. Then Elisabetta, Leonardo and Salvatore rode for Podgorica, to rejoin the queen at the equinox.

2nd Story: A Skin to Dance In

On Sunday 7 March 1300 Queen Mária, Mateo, Pavo and Casimir set out on a forced march to Trgovište, to learn the fate of her younger sister, Erzsebet. On the way Mária told them that Salvatore had "executed" Garvyn on her orders. In Podgorica, the largest market town of continental Travunia, the children were preparing to drown the Morana dolls at the end of winter, and the townsfolk argued in the church over whether the festival was the work of Satan, for six children had been taken during it in the last decade and never found; people said they were sold as slaves in Ragusa. There they met the tsupan Konstantin, an honest, hard-working man who cares for the villagers of his ward and is critical of King Milutin's war against the Greeks, who warned them not to get lost at Bratimin Vir.

On Monday 8 March Casimir dreamed that the queen took him alone to the ruins of Doclea, where a straw Morana bled, holding two babies whose flesh merged in Mária's hands. The party crossed the Bjelasica pass and the Tara, Mária steering the talk away from her family, and in Berane the old innkeeper Jovanna served them a whole pig and would not take payment. They slept in her barn, where the queen slept naked and badly, and what happened in the barn stays in the barn.

On Tuesday 9 March Casimir dreamed of a wolf seizing Mateo by the neck, and Mateo woke very sore. The stable boy Marco warned them that the caves of Bratimin Vir were dangerous, and the healer Anastasija gave Mateo something for the pain and a pillow for his saddle. In the Tifran gorge Casimir and Pavo found a pack of eight wolves eating a man alive and, retreating, saw a wolf the size of a bear. At the monastery they found Erzsebet's grave, recently disturbed and once tended by two young girls: "Here lies the inobedient consort of the king who died on the second winter of the reign of king Uroš". The monk Vlad called her a whore who slept with all the king's men. A hooded figure leading a horse found them there: Konstantin, Milutin's son, who was looking for Erzsebet's daughters, his half-sisters Ana-Neda and Zorica, rumoured to live in the mountains above Podgorica and to be preparing a ritual to steal young boys. Mária confessed that she was Erzsebet's sister.

On Wednesday 10 March Casimir dreamed of Milutin as a wolf tearing out Mateo's throat and raping the queen. Konstantin confessed that he believed his father was the Antichrist, and they all agreed to find and save his half-sisters. The presbyter of the monastery told Casimir that Stefan Uroš had killed the wolf and taken his skin and his power, so that ever since all the wolves hate the people; that they would be safe only on the path of the Lord, not following the goat, whose milk is the blood of Satan; and that the king's bastard daughters had made a cult of children worshipping Satan. Casimir also heard that the witch of Podgorica had turned into two girls to corrupt the people, and that whomever the king of the wolves looks in the eye is his next victim.

On the way back the wolves ate Mateo's horse and cornered the party in a cave with a bear; Konstantin panicked and galloped back to Berane with Mateo. In Berane the wolves turned into men and summoned them, with the healer Anastasija, to the church, where Milutin, the big bad wolf, assaulted the queen, threatened to strip them naked and chase them through the woods, and drew a sword on Mateo. Casimir struck at the king's sides while Pavo cut off his hands, which became wolf paws, and the king fled as a wolf. On the bridge Milutin tore off Konstantin's knee, and Pavo cut off the wolf's head, which flew back onto its body; the wolves retreated, setting fire to the south of the village. As the group left the church, one of the henchmen had beheaded Anastasija, whom Konstantin loved. By morning his knee had healed. That night Casimir saw Milutin tell the story of the day to a black-clad priest, who wrote it in his book, dipping his pen in Anastasija's severed head.

On Thursday 11 March they buried Anastasija, Mateo gave a rousing speech calling the villagers to act against Milutin, and Konstantin roused Metasovo to march on Berane. On the road to Podgorica five wolves ambushed them: Casimir was severely wounded and Mateo was carried off to Milutin for "interrogation". On the way two lost sisters met Konstantin and took the party to their hovel in the woods, where they treated Casimir.

On Friday 12 March, the vernal equinox, Pavo and Mária rode to Podgorica and met Leonardo, who reported that Salvatore had gone towards the ruins the day before and never returned. At the Byzantines' monastery Pavo and Leonardo met Zoe, who knew that Milutin was a monster and had a plan against him, and at lunch the innkeeper Olga warned them that everyone who goes to the Metochia to be healed comes back changed. Back at the hovel Casimir told of his dream: Hamelin, a polite man dressed as a Venetian merchant, had opened a chained door to a stair going down; an old woman sitting on Erzsebet's grave gave him a potion that, dropped into Milutin's wounds, would stop them healing more than once; in Ras he freed Mateo, crucified, tortured and faithless, and met Queen Ana, who led them out into the forest, where, running as wolves, they found the real king of the wolves, skinless and chained, who told them, "Finally some souls which have not yet surrendered and will save us". Konstantin hushed him, for Milutin can know what he knows.

That night, feeling the presence of the goat, the group went to the ruins of Doclea, where one of the sisters prayed to Morana to take her power and control the seasons, while the girls danced around the fire and maypole and the boys wore Roman togas and nothing else. Fearing the children would be lost as in previous years, Pavo dragged her away from her prayer; she cried that he had ruined everything, the children lay around them seemingly violated as if much time had passed, and her eyes bled until Pavo and Leonardo brought her back to her sister. The twins wept that they had betrayed God for Satan and now had betrayed Satan too. At the inn Mária introduced Zoe as Angelina, duchess of Thessaly, and the Varangian as Stefan Uroš Dečanski, Milutin's firstborn son.

The plan was set: Elisabetta, posing as Zoe, and Leonardo, as her tutor, would bring Simonis to Ras in the carriage with Dečanski; Simonis would poison Milutin, Dečanski would slay him, and Casimir and Pavo would enter by a secondary gate during the wedding, while Mária and Angelina took the twins and Konstantin to Ostrog and Stupovi. Angelina told Casimir that when the founder of a wolf pack dies, all the wounds dealt by his pack return, but that he would be protected by wearing the wolf's skin. At the ruined hovel Casimir went down a hole into a cave with a skeleton, likely Erzsebet's, and found himself, with Pavo, in the dungeons of Ras; time had passed. The carriage passed Berane, destroyed and burned, all its villagers killed, and Leonardo woke at Ras with Dečanski. In the courtyard Milutin skinned Mateo and Queen Ana while the court watched; they burned the skins, ate their bodies and drank their blood. On the wedding night they waited while Milutin raped Simonis, to get her with child, which was impossible, and then pleased himself with Elisabetta in front of her, a harrowing scene for both women. Then Dečanski, Pavo, Casimir and Leonardo killed him, Dečanski set the fortress on fire, and Leonardo barely escaped the burning building with his ancient texts. Casimir, wearing the wolf's skin, was spared when the wounds returned. Dečanski is now the new "emperor" of Serbia.

At the tavern of Trgovište, below Ras, Casimir met Ludmila, a barmaid who moonlighted as a whore for Milutin's tsupans and was drawn to his wolf-self. When Casimir, as the wolf, ate a man there, she left for Dubrovnik, where she sold herself as a sex-slave; only after she had gone did he realise that the man had been her estranged father.

Later Casimir rode south to Shkodër, where Konstantin and his twin half-sisters had taken refuge, and gave the nuns gold from Mária to help her nieces return to the path of God. He and Konstantin bonded further over all they had been through together.

3rd Story: Weathering the Storm

In Ilok Ion brought ispán Ugrin Csák his winter report from Đakovo. Charles Robert planned to land at Salona in May, join the Šubić forces at Bribir, take reinforcements from the Frankopán lords of Krk, march inland to Zagreb to join the Babonić, follow the Sava downstream to join Ugrin in July or August, and then go north to take the crown from the "bastard" king András III in Buda. Venetian merchants said that his grandmother and the papal legate were already travelling through Serbia. The heretic church of Bosnia was gaining control over the Catholic bishop of Đakovo and was rumoured to have joined forces with Albertino Morosini, whom the bastard king had made Ban of all Croatia, and Albertino was to be received by Ban Stjepan Kotromanić, Ugrin's ally along with Dragutin, for reasons no one could uncover.

Ugrin sent his son, Miklós, under the protection of Álmos, Ion and Stanislav, to deliver a letter to his bastard, Bishop Vencel of Mitrovica; to have Vencel and Miklós send Vladislav, the firstborn of Dragutin and Katalina and Ugrin's tax-collector in Mitrovica, on an embassy to the Kotromanić reaffirming the old alliance; to reaffirm Dragutin's loyalty to it; and to bring the Queen of Naples and the papal legate under his "protection" in Ilok. In Mitrovica Vencel, devastated by his father's letter, confessed to Miklós that he, not Dragutin, was the father of Urošica, and that Ugrin had ordered him to imply that Dragutin's older children, Vladislav and Jelisaveta, were bastards like her. Urošica revealed that she had found the journal of the late Archbishop Ugrin Csák of Kalocsa, describing a ritual in the ancient ruins of Sirmium that had granted him a great weapon or tool against the Tatars almost a century ago; Vladislav confiscated it, and Ion saw both their shadows shrink in the sunlight. Ion also failed spectacularly to seduce anyone in broad daylight, earning an unenviable reputation among the peasants, and Vencel's house stood deserted, with Katalina suspiciously absent.

In Beograd Dragutin, gathering an army against Milutin or the Bulgar hordes, received the embassy at once, alone but for a little boy. Loyal to the old alliance and suspicious of the Neapolitan, papal and Roman embassies converging on Ras, he offered to arrange an audience with Milutin so that Miklós and his companions could investigate further. Everyone wondered how he had restored the walls of Beograd and filled his White Keep with Byzantine opulence in lands ravaged by civil war and the steppe hordes for a century. Stanislav left by night, with Miklós's agreement, to report to Ugrin, and Álmos went out for a last night of depraved excess. The group weighed their next moves, mindful of Ugrin's fallback plan, which Vencel had accepted: if Queen Mária's line failed, to reveal Katalina's children as Ugrin's own bastards.

Back in Mitrovica, a town built on Roman ruins and covered in unnatural darkness, Ion saw a tall hooded figure climb quickly over Vencel's walls, and inside the house the group found bloody entrails laid out on the dining table, which they took outside and burned. In the cathedral Vencel told them that Urošica was dead and that he would bury Mateo later. At the inn a scream in the night led them to a young woman standing over a bleeding corpse, and they learned that the town was terrorised by a killer: five murders and six disappearances, and someone had risen from the grave after an Orthodox burial. At Vladislav's house Ion recognised him as the hooded figure, and Vladislav arrested the party until Miklós ordered Ion's release.

Old Mihály, the village madman, told them of the swamp lord of Macsó, mentally unstable, who lives in a mausoleum and commands the dead, and claimed that Urošica led the undead. Father Sava, the Orthodox episcope, was of no help, but the gravedigger showed them Urošica's grave, its soil unusually fresh beside the sealed grave of the archbishop. Devastated, Miklós insisted on visiting his sister's grave that night against all advice; there the group saw Vladislav and two strangers standing silently before it, a large white owl diving into the grave, and Urošica rising to feed on a corpse with them.

Afterwards Mihály told Leonardo and Pavo of Comptor Siward, lost and corrupted, whose Transylvanian chapter in the Burzenland had lost touch with the others and become a secret order, fought by the Saxons and the Magyars but not the Székelys, and said that Mitrovica was lost to the ancient evil unleashed there, for which the lord of the swamp was to blame. When figures began walking from the graveyard towards the town, Ion had the priests ring all the bells. While Mária, Elisabetta and Miklós held the inn, Pavo, Leonardo, Ion and Casimir climbed into Vladislav's house, found half-eaten human organs, and fought him when he attacked; he lost an arm and his life, and two other figures escaped without a trace. The feeding recalled what they had seen on Vis, and the house began to flood as they fled. Katalina was found in the old Benedictine monastery outside Mitrovica, devoured by a huge tree.

Until the “Return” of the King

From April to November 1300, with Queen Mária brought to safety, the group at last had time for their own goals while the army of Charles Robert made its way to the domain of Ugrin Csák. Casimir went south.

After Mitrovica Leonardo tried to dream of Rozafa and saw her caught in the tree that had devoured Katalina at the monastery, unable to speak, as if held by the evil released from the mausoleum; he shared it with Casimir, asking him to keep it secret for now. He proposed to take Vladislav's place, but Mária suggested instead that he become constable of Mitrovica, with some authority but without too great a debt to Ugrin, or the burden of bureaucracy and tax-collecting. Miklós agreed to speak to Ugrin, provided Leonardo reported to him weekly on his investigation of Urošica. That night Leonardo saw a trail of rot running from the monastery's tree towards Beograd, as if the lord of the swamp had trapped Rozafa with him. Ugrin agreed, though Mária disliked his going to Miklós.

Leonardo returned to Mitrovica with Ugrin's letter, and Vencel installed him in the old episcopal palace. At the inn he met Sandra of Aquela, a well-dressed but weary Venetian woman travelling alone in the Balkans, who seemed to know more than she said and avoided any talk of spirits and satanic powers. He invited her to stay at the palace but refused her Urošica's room, and by morning she was gone, leaving a letter: "I will wait for you in Beograd. I will give you one more chance. You have ten days." Finding a corpse linked to cannibalism in the residence, he carried it wrapped in a sheet through the streets to the frightened Father Sava, who arranged its burial with Vencel, and the town noticed both the woman and the corpse.

Mihály, the eccentric Teutonic alchemist, discussed Urošica's disappearance with him: she might have gone to a relative among the heretics of Bosnia, to Beograd seeking Dragutin, whom she believed to be her father, or with the heretical troupe linked to Sandra, and two of the three roads led to Beograd. At the monastery Leonardo burned the rotting tree with liquid fire, and much of the monastery ruins with it; whether Rozafa was there remains unknown. On his way back Mihály gave him a book of Hermes Trismegistus, full of forbidden knowledge and hard to put down.

As constable Leonardo worked to win the trust of the town and to mend his relations with Vencel and Father Sava, and over a friendly dinner they agreed to begin a new port with two piers and to ask Ugrin for help, although his first official letter was not quite a success. A black stain began to mark his hands as he read the book. He rode to Beograd and, at a costly inn by the Sava full of Venetians and Morosini, met Sandra again and heard that Dragutin and Dečanski were preparing to attack Bulgaria. Sandra had him pay a fortune for a meeting with Larissa, a famous fifteen-year-old performer under Miloš, who passed as her protector and father, then revealed to her that Miloš had killed her family, cursed her village and taken her to exploit her, gave her a key to his journal hidden under his bed, and vanished through the window. Leonardo offered Larissa refuge and protection in Mitrovica. He also learned that Urošica was probably in Beograd and might now drink blood, while young Serbian men were dying there one each night.

Pavo and Ion went to Ilok, where Mária told them that Voivode László Kán had exiled the Transylvanian Teutons, though she had been too young to know more and Elisabetta might. Pavo had to deliver a letter from the Pope to the archbishop of Kalocsa by Christmas, and Mária had promised to help him in return for his absolute loyalty. Ion asked leave to visit his father's grave, and Ugrin was not enthusiastic. Kalocsa proved a small town of 500 people with an oversized cathedral, whose monks see themselves as the last bastion of Christianity before a sea of pagans. Archbishop János Hont-Pázmány opened the letter: a ticket for two to the Bishop of Alba Iulia, with an order to rebuild the Teutonic chapter of Transylvania.

Crossing the marshes of the Cuman plain, they were "abandoned" at dawn by their guide, János, and five riders let them pass for 950 silver and their horses. At Szeged the ferryman raised his price and made an enemy or two, and they crossed by boat to the harbour. Vlach peasants told them that they did not trust the Hungarian Catholics, that the king sent no money to repair the town, and that Ban Tivadar Vejtehi of Temesvár and Severin ruled as far as Lipova, and the abbot of the Franciscan monastery told them that many Cumans were now baptised and profited from the trade in salt and animals, that Catholics were safe for now, that the castle walls were restored, and that some who entered Déva never came out.

At Csanád, which Ion had fled as a boy when László's men sacked it, Ion touched the walls of his old room and heard his father's voice: "Avenge me". Bishop Antal invited him to become a priest and keep the room, and told them how, sixty years ago, before he was voivode, László had besieged Feldioara, the last holding of the Teutonic Knights in Transylvania, on the orders of King Béla IV, and staked the last of the Teutons with their Comptor, perhaps as a sacrifice to the devil. László has two sons, Kálmán and Kocsoh, and a daughter, Ladiszla, and is supported by the Saxon, Hungarian and Székely nobles; he could lose his power if he lost his contacts and allies. The bishop offered to help free the people from László's evil rule, so long as the people of Csanád were never put at risk, and swore them both to secrecy.

At his father's unmarked grave Ion read the shadows and was drawn into a dark realm, under a darker sun and in silence, into a church where a huge faceless statue, its head a dark rippling lake, wrote in a book before kneeling human statues. One of them was his father, Bishop Bereck, who told him that László had trapped him in this devil's world; Ion swore to trap László there in his turn and free his father's spirit to Heaven. Apart from the bishop, only a "foreign relative" of his father, Lőrinc Telegdi, knew of the grave. When Ion took Pavo into the shadows too, they found the monastery burning with a sickly blue flame and the cathedral chained shut, and a knight in full plate with a dragon helm, an upside-down cross and a spear dripping blood, who named himself "your end", impaled Pavo and asked whether he would be saved by God or by his blade. Pavo chose God and died slowly, and woke holding Ion's hand at the grave, with an old scar where the spear had pierced him.

In Arad they found the town celebrating the sowing, with the widow of the previous leader among the notables, and at Lipova they slept at the house of Gellért the smith. Déva proved a village no bigger than Lipova beside an oversized harbour and an even more oversized fortress on a volcano, its people scared and its guards suspicious. At the inn, the Blood of the Grape, the servant girls told them that the derelict mansion in the village centre had burned thirty years before, once a rich Saxon family's and now the voivode's, and Father Dan warned people away from it. Ioan, son of Knez Koste and brother of Irina, had his own room in the bunkers, where they could stay. Then Leonardo arrived and told the guards that he was looking for a Teutonic knight, and that one was here, and the three fled on horseback, reaching Alba Iulia the next midday. There Pavo gave the letter to the bishop and received an open letter with a golden bull declaring that he is on a papal mission; the bishop also told them that many women of Alba Iulia are the sluts of Satan and need to be tortured and burned.
