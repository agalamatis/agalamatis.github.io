The eighteen eras, by number of entries

All 22 of Türkiye’s World Heritage properties are tagged, including the newest, Sardis and the Lydian Tumuli of Bin Tepe (2025). Türkiye also has one of the longest tentative lists in the world — 80 entries — of which 74 are represented here, 23 of them added as rows of their own marked “not in Lonely Planet”. Six are named in the markdown file instead, because their UNESCO reference numbers could not be read reliably. No Turkish property has ever been delisted. The era list supplied has heavy overlaps, resolved by region and cue together. Among the ancient bands, a site on the Greek-settled coasts founded between 1100 and 331 BC is Ancient Greek; inland and before 547 BC it is Bronze Age, which in this list is the band that holds Hittite, Phrygian, Urartian and Lydian Anatolia — Hattuşa, Gordion, Sardis, Troy, Van. Among the medieval bands, Istanbul and Marmara stay Byzantine until 1453 while the Anatolian interior turns Seljuk from 1037, and the Latin orders and maritime republics are picked out by name and coast. Achaemenid and Parthian are empty and kept rather than dropped: Persian Anatolia left nothing Lonely Planet lists in its own right, and the Parthians never held Anatolia proper. The Roman band runs to AD 610 as specified, so it also holds Late Antiquity — the Hagia Sophia at İznik (532) and Sümela’s foundation (386) sit in it. There is no Arab or Umayyad band, so the Umayyad Grand Mosque at Harran falls inside the Byzantine span by date. Three systematic estimates are worth knowing: an undated church, rock-cut valley, cistern or underground city is filed as Byzantine; an undated medrese, caravanserai or türbe in the interior as Seljuk and everything else Islamic as Classical Ottoman; an undated ancient city as Roman on the coasts and Hellenistic inland. The regions are the official geographical seven, not the tourist ones, so Hattuşa, Amasya and Safranbolu are in the Black Sea region, Çanakkale and Troy in Marmara, and Fethiye and Bodrum in the Aegean. Turkish sight names repeat relentlessly — there are 17 separate Ulu Camis here — so every Wikipedia candidate was checked against its own opening paragraph and 68 false matches were dropped, plus 10 names covering different buildings in different towns. Founding dates come from Wikidata and from the opening paragraph of each article, with excavation years and mound dates discarded; 31 entries were set by hand. The area inscriptions — the Historic Areas of Istanbul, Cappadocia, Safranbolu, Bursa, the Diyarbakır walls — are tagged at destination level. Map links are searches, not pins: Lonely Planet publishes no coordinates.
