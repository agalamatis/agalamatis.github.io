All 8 of Hungary’s World Heritage properties are tagged — 6 matched to existing Lonely Planet rows, 2 added as rows of their own — and 7 of the 11 tentative-list entries are represented, marked WHS-T. Hungary has never had a property delisted, so there is no former-site category. The Budapest property covers the Danube banks, the Castle Quarter and Andrássy Avenue rather than the whole city, so it is tagged attraction by attraction. Coverage is thin outside the capital: 181 of the 383 Lonely Planet rows are in Budapest, and six counties have no attractions in the index at all. Rows are assigned to counties by town, not by Lonely Planet’s own regional pages. Sources: Lonely Planet’s points-of-interest index, the UNESCO World Heritage List and English Wikipedia — 40 English-title matches that turned out to be generic concepts or foreign namesakes were dropped after checking each article against its own opening paragraph. Founding dates come from Wikidata and from the opening paragraph of each Wikipedia article; rows with no article of their own are dated by site type, which is an estimate rather than a source, and in Hungary that estimate is usually Habsburg. There is no Ottoman band, so the mosques and türbe of Pécs, Eger and Buda sit in Habsburg by date — filter by the Mosque or Islamic site type to find them.
