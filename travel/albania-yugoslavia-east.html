The thirteen eras, by number of entries

All 12 World Heritage properties with territory in these five countries are tagged and all 26 tentative-list entries are represented. Thirteen entries have no Lonely Planet listing and were added as rows of their own, marked “not in Lonely Planet” — the three beech-forest components, the Lower Selca tombs, Bashtovë, Kurbinovo, Caričin Grad, Slatinski Izvor, the Deliblato Sands, the Šar Mountains, Biogradska Gora and the Zvezda beech reserve on Tara. Kosovo is not a State Party to the Convention and has no tentative list of its own; its four medieval monuments are inscribed under Serbia as Medieval Monuments in Kosovo, on the List of World Heritage in Danger since 2006 — the only property here with that status. No property in any of the five countries has ever been delisted. Two bands were added to the era list supplied: Medieval (1180–1479), for everything medieval that was never Serbian, Bulgarian or Byzantine — the Albanian principalities, Zeta under the Balšići, Krujë, the stećci of Durmitor; and the Serbian band taken as the whole medieval Serbian state, 1166–1459, rather than the imperial decades alone. Venetian is applied only on the Adriatic coast of Montenegro and Albania, with real per-town windows — Kotor from 1420, Herceg Novi only from 1687, Bar and Ulcinj ending in 1571, Durrës in 1501 — so Krujë, Theth and the Lake Skadar shore are excluded. Two gaps in the era list were filled rather than left blank: there is no Habsburg band, so Petrovaradin, Subotica and Belgrade’s Austrian-built magazine sit in Ottoman, which here means the early-modern centuries 1479–1878 rather than the ruling power; and the Modern band is entered at independence — 1878 for Serbia and Montenegro, 1912 for the other three — rather than 1918, so that Cetinje’s legations and Belgrade’s nineteenth-century cathedrals are not filed as Ottoman. Coverage is very uneven: Montenegro has 209 rows and Kosovo 28. A direct Wikipedia lookup on these names returns many generic articles and foreign namesakes — borscht for the village of Borsh, Niagara Falls for a weir outside Podgorica, the Blue Grotto of Capri — so every candidate was checked against its own opening paragraph and 40 false matches were dropped, plus 2 Lonely Planet names that cover different buildings in different towns. Founding dates come from Wikidata and from the opening paragraph of each Wikipedia article; rows with no article of their own — 293 of 508 — are dated by site type and region, which is an estimate rather than a source, and 66 were set by hand. The town inscriptions — Berat and Gjirokastra, the Kotor region, the Ohrid region, Stari Ras — are tagged at destination level. Map links are searches, not pins: Lonely Planet publishes no coordinates.
