The nine eras, by number of entries

All 17 of Czechia’s World Heritage properties are tagged and all 14 of its tentative-list entries are represented. Ten inscribed properties and three tentative entries were matched to Lonely Planet listings; the other seven inscribed properties and eleven tentative entries have no Lonely Planet listing at all and were added as rows of their own, marked “not in Lonely Planet” — Zelená Hora, Holašovice, Litomyšl, the Jizera beech forests, the Jáchymov mining landscape, Žatec and the Saaz hops, and Kladruby nad Labem among them. Czechia has never had a property delisted, so there is no former-site category. The Historic Centre of Prague covers Hradčany, Malá Strana, the Old and New Towns and Vyšehrad, so those five neighbourhoods are tagged in full and the outer districts are not; the Olomouc inscription is the Holy Trinity Column alone, not the town. Rows are assigned to the fourteen kraje by town; Lonely Planet reaches ten of them, and the World Heritage rows bring in the other four. Prague alone holds 217 of the 384 rows. Founding dates come from Wikidata and from the opening paragraph of each Wikipedia article; rows with no article of their own — 220 of 384 — are dated by site type, which is an estimate rather than a source. Where the bands overlap, Great Moravia (833–907) takes the Moravian regions and the Přemyslid band takes Bohemia from 870. Map links are searches, not pins: Lonely Planet publishes no coordinates.
