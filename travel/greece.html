The fourteen eras, by number of entries

All 21 of Greece’s World Heritage properties are tagged, including the two newest — Minoan Palatial Centres (2025) and the wider area of Mount Olympus (2026) — and all 11 tentative-list entries. Only the Samaria Gorge has no Lonely Planet listing and was added as a row of its own, marked “not in Lonely Planet”. No Greek property has ever been delisted. The era list has four overlaps, resolved here by region and date together: Crete and the Cyclades stay Minoan until 1450 BC while the mainland turns Mycenaean from 1750 BC; Dark Ages and Archaic split at 800 BC, Archaic and Classical at 500 BC, Hellenistic and Roman at 146 BC; and Byzantine, Frankish & Venetian and Ottoman are decided by the real conquest year of each region, so a 1400 building is Venetian on Crete, Byzantine at Mystras and Ottoman in Thessaloniki. Ottoman gives way to Kingdom at each region’s own liberation — 1797 in the Ionian islands, 1830–33 in the south, 1881 in Thessaly, 1898 on Crete, 1912–13 in the north, 1947 in the Dodecanese. The Roman band runs to AD 610 as specified, so it also holds the Early Christian centuries: Thessaloniki’s Rotunda, Hosios David and Agios Dimitrios sit in it rather than in Byzantine. One systematic estimate is worth knowing: a church or monastery with no Wikipedia article of its own — 170 rows — is filed as Byzantine, which is right for most Greek ecclesiastical foundations but wrong for post-Byzantine village churches. Coverage is uneven — Crete 250 rows, Eastern Macedonia and Thrace 38 — and Lonely Planet itemises interiors, with 18 entries inside Knossos and 37 on Delos. A direct Wikipedia lookup on Greek sight names is unusually unreliable, because there are 36 Archaeological Museums and 14 Kastros here and because the lookup returns namesakes abroad — Hagia Sophia in Istanbul, St John’s Gate in Clerkenwell — so every candidate was checked against its own opening paragraph and 61 false matches were dropped, plus 12 names that cover different places in different towns. Founding dates come from Wikidata and from the opening paragraph of each Wikipedia article, with excavation and rebuilding years discarded rather than used; 42 entries were set by hand. The area inscriptions — the Acropolis, Delos, the Medieval City of Rhodes, the Old Town of Corfu, Meteora, Mount Athos, the Minoan palaces — are tagged at destination level. Map links are searches, not pins: Lonely Planet publishes no coordinates.
