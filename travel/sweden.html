The ten eras, by number of entries

Built from Lonely Planet’s own points-of-interest index for Sweden — 547 listings — across 115 destinations and all 25 historical provinces. The regions are Stockholm plus Götaland, Svealand and Norrland, the three lands, with the landskap as a second filter. That is not Lonely Planet’s own chapter structure, which mixes lands with provinces and leaves fifty-odd towns outside any chapter; two of its filings are corrected here, Karlskrona from Småland to Blekinge and Göteborg to Västergötland. All 15 inscribed World Heritage properties are tagged and the single tentative-list entry, The Rise of Systematic Biology, carried by the Linnaean sites in Uppsala. The Struve Geodetic Arc has no Lonely Planet listing at its Swedish survey points and was added as a row marked “not in Lonely Planet”. The High Coast is shared with Finland’s Kvarken Archipelago and the Struve arc with nine other states. Sweden has never had a property delisted, so there is no former-site category here. Area inscriptions are tagged at destination level, which is why the Hanseatic Town of Visby shows twelve rows; Norrköping’s Hällristningsmuseet is deliberately not tagged with the Tanum inscription, since its carvings are at Himmelstalund, 300km away. On the eras: a sight sits in the band its founding year falls in, not the band of the fabric standing today, and a museum is dated by its own founding rather than by what it holds — the Vasa Museum is World & Cold Wars, the ship inside it is not. One exception is recorded: **Birka** was founded about 750, so a strict reading would put Sweden’s great Viking town in Prehistory; its occupation and significance are ninth- and tenth-century, so it is filed in the Viking Age. Your Prehistory band runs 12,000 BC to AD 800 as one span, so the Tanum rock carvings of about 1700 BC and the Gamla Uppsala mounds of about AD 500 share it, with no Bronze Age or Iron Age band to separate them. The Kalmar Union band carries three entries. That is not a gap in the data: union-era Sweden built little that survives as a visitor sight, and the buildings that straddle 1397–1521 are dated either side of it — the Gammelstad church town, consecrated 1492, is the clearest case and sits here. At the other end, Modern takes 76 entries, a strikingly large share, because Swedish museum and gallery building since 1991 has been relentless. 328 entries take their era from their type alone: an undated church, cathedral, abbey or city gate is Middle Ages; an undated castle, fort, old town or mine is Independence; an undated manor, harbour, square or garden is Enlightenment; an undated ironworks, lighthouse, canal, railway or civic building is Liberalisation; an undated museum, open-air museum, statue or theme park is World & Cold Wars; an art gallery, shop or Sámi centre is Modern. A further 50 natural features are Natural by type, 166 eras come from a Wikidata date, 41 from the opening paragraph of the sight’s own article, nine from a period cue, and three were set by hand. Wikipedia was resolved by direct lookup, then the langlinks of the Swedish Wikipedia — which is what turns Livrustkammaren, Jamtli and Ájtte into their English articles and Stora Synagogan into the Great Synagogue of Stockholm — then Swedish-to-English name forms (Domkyrka to Cathedral, Slott to Castle, Gruva to Mine, Fästning to Fortress), then a full-text search. Every candidate was checked against its own opening paragraph for a Swedish cue, which caught Abisko’s Aurora Sky Station resolving to a BBC nature series, Käringön to a regional football association, and a long tail of sights resolving to “Listed buildings in X County”; eleven picks were repaired by hand. English coverage of small Swedish sights is thin, so 229 of 548 rows have an article of their own and the rest point at the town, the municipality or the institution that covers them, shown with a lower-case w. Map links are searches, not pins: Lonely Planet publishes no coordinates.
