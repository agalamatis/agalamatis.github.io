The thirteen eras, by number of entries

Built from Lonely Planet’s own points-of-interest indexes for Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania — 647 listings, 221 Estonian, 188 Latvian and 244 Lithuanian — across 116 destinations. The regions are Tallinn, Riga and Vilnius pulled out, then the historical ones: Northern Estonia, Southern Estonia and the Estonian Islands; Vidzeme, Latgale, Zemgale and Kurzeme; Aukštaitija, Žemaitija and Dzūkija. **Suvalkija** is the fourteenth region and it is empty: Lonely Planet lists nothing on the left bank of the Nemunas, so the region is named here and carries no rows. Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit are filed under Žemaitija as you asked, which means their Prussian and German past — Memel was not Lithuanian until 1923 — shows in the era column rather than in the region column: Klaipėda Castle, founded by the Livonian Order in 1252, sits in Northern Crusades. Two of the region labels do work the era bands depend on. **Vidzeme** is the Swedish Livonia band’s territory, **Latgale** is Polish Livonia’s, and **Kurzeme with Zemgale** is the Duchy of Courland’s — which is why the six territorial eras can overlap in time without ever colliding on a single sight. All 8 inscribed World Heritage properties are tagged — Tallinn, Riga, Kuldīga, Vilnius, the Curonian Spit, Kernavė, Modernist Kaunas and the Struve arc — and all 7 tentative-list entries. The Struve Geodetic Arc has only one Lonely Planet listing at its nine Baltic survey points, the Tartu Old Observatory, so the other eight were added as five rows marked “not in Lonely Planet”; the Grobiņa archaeological ensemble was added the same way. The Curonian Spit is inscribed jointly with Russia and the Struve arc with nine other states. None of the three has ever had a property delisted, so there is no former-site category here. Area inscriptions are tagged at destination level — which is why Tallinn’s Old Town shows 30 rows, Riga’s 25 and Kuldīga’s 18 — with one exception. **Vilnius** is tagged on its named Old Town components instead, because Lonely Planet files all 88 Vilnius sights under a single heading and many of them — Paneriai, the TV Tower, Europos Parkas, the cemeteries — lie well outside the inscribed area. On the eras: a sight sits in the band its founding year falls in, not the band of the fabric standing today, and a museum is dated by its own founding rather than by what it holds. The six territorial bands resolve by region, so a 1400 sight in Riga is Livonian Confederation and a 1400 sight in Vilnius is Grand Duchy of Lithuania. Sights founded between 1198 and 1290 go to **Northern Crusades**, the shared band, even though the Confederation and the Grand Duchy are dated from 1207 and 1236 on the list. One correction to the agreed dates: the Swedish–Russian seam is set at **1710**, when Riga and Tallinn both fell, rather than at the Treaty of Nystad in 1721 — otherwise Peter the Great’s Kadriorg, begun in 1718 in a city already Russian for eight years, would be filed under Swedish rule. Three bands are nearly empty. **Prehistory** holds three and the **Viking Age** three. Both are real in the ground — the Baltic hill forts, the Scandinavian colony at Grobiņa, the Iron Age cemeteries — but Lonely Planet lists almost none of it as a visitable site, and what it lists it lists as museums, which are dated by their own founding. **Swedish Livonia & Estonia** holds six, which is thinner than Sweden’s 150 years there deserves: Tartu’s university of 1632 and Tallinn’s bastions are here, but most of what Sweden built was rebuilt under the tsars and is dated accordingly. Note too that merging your old bands 5–7 means **1918–1940 now sits inside Russian Empire**, so the Freedom Monument of 1935 and the interwar republics are filed under the empire that had just collapsed. 357 entries take their era from their type alone — a high share, and the reason is worth stating: 372 of 653 rows have no English Wikipedia article of their own, and a sight is dated only from its own article, never from the town’s. Without that rule the Kuldīga synagogue took the town’s date of 1378 and the Jūrmala museums took 1785. The type defaults: an undated church is medieval in Estonia, Riga and Vidzeme, where the Livonian stone churches survive, but early-modern in Courland, Latgale and Lithuania, where the surviving churches are overwhelmingly baroque; an undated cathedral, monastery, castle, city gate, old town or tower is the medieval band; an undated palace, manor house, square or old house is the early-modern band; an undated park, works, lighthouse, bridge, railway, cemetery, spa, fort, harbour or theatre is Russian Empire; an undated museum, open-air museum, zoo, ship or sports ground is Occupation; an art gallery, market, statue or theme park is Modern; a hill fort is Viking Age; 66 natural features are Natural. 139 eras come from a Wikidata date, 59 from the opening paragraph of the sight’s own article, four from a period cue, and 22 were set by hand. Period cues are deliberately **not** applied to museums and visitor centres. Wikipedia was resolved by direct lookup, then the langlinks of the Estonian, Latvian and Lithuanian Wikipedias, then Baltic-to-English name forms, then a full-text search. The middle two waves between them returned **17 articles out of 626 titles**, and the reason is the mirror image of the Nordic countries: Lonely Planet gives Baltic sights in English — “Town Hall Square”, “Fat Margaret”, “St Anne’s Church” — so there is no native title to follow a langlink from. Every candidate was then checked against its own opening paragraph for a Baltic cue and rejected without one, which caught Town Hall resolving to the general article on city halls, Bastion Hill to a feature in Antarctica, Patarei to the electric battery, Nuku to a province of Ghana, and Jacob’s Barracks to Treblinka. 98 picks were repaired by hand. 281 of 653 rows have an article of their own and the rest point at the town, the neighbourhood or the institution that covers them, shown with a lower-case w. Map links are searches, not pins: Lonely Planet publishes no coordinates.
