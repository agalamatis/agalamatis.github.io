The thirteen eras, by number of entries

All 17 World Heritage properties across the three countries are tagged and all 28 tentative-list entries are represented. Fifteen inscribed properties and twenty tentative entries were matched to Lonely Planet listings; the rest have no Lonely Planet listing and were added as rows of their own, marked “not in Lonely Planet” — the Ljubljana Marsh pile dwellings, the Vjetrenica cave, the Fužina hills, Velebit, the Split extension, the Primošten vineyards, Blidinje, Stolac, Perućica and the Sarajevo Jewish cemetery. Three properties are shared with neighbouring states: the beech forests, the stećci graveyards and the Venetian Works of Defence. None of the three countries has ever had a property delisted, so there is no former-site category. Two eras are empty — Illyrian and Avar: Lonely Planet lists no Illyrian hillfort as a sight in its own right and nothing Avar at all. The bands are kept rather than dropped. The Ottoman band, 1463–1878, was added to the era list because without it every mosque, tekke, han and Ottoman bridge in Bosnia would have been filed as Habsburg on the strength of its date alone; the Military Frontier counties of Lika-Senj, Karlovac and Sisak-Moslavina are the Habsburg side of that frontier and are not treated as Ottoman. The Venetian band is applied only on the Adriatic coast between 1409 and 1797 — an inland building of the same date goes to Medieval or Habsburg. Árpád, 1102–1301, is the personal union with Hungary for Croatia; for Slovenia the same window was Babenberg and early Habsburg rule inside the Holy Roman Empire, so there the band is a date range rather than a polity. Coverage is uneven — Croatia 499 rows, Slovenia 364, Bosnia & Herzegovina only 101 — and Lonely Planet’s own groupings (“Dalmatia”, “Karst & Coast”, “Southern Bosnia & Hercegovina”) cut across the administrative regions, so 60 sights filed that way were placed by naming the town each stands in. Founding dates come from Wikidata and from the opening paragraph of each Wikipedia article; rows with no article of their own — 740 of 964 — are dated by site type and region, which is an estimate rather than a source. A direct Wikipedia lookup on these names returns many generic articles and foreign namesakes, so every candidate was checked against its own opening paragraph and 82 false matches were dropped. The urban inscriptions — Dubrovnik, Split, Trogir, the Mostar old bridge area — are tagged at destination level. Map links are searches, not pins: Lonely Planet publishes no coordinates.
