All 18 of Poland’s World Heritage properties are tagged — 10 matched to existing Lonely Planet rows, 8 added as rows of their own — and 4 of the 8 tentative-list entries are represented, marked WHS-T. Poland has never had a property delisted, so there is no former-site category. Rows are assigned to voivodeships by town, not by Lonely Planet’s own regional pages, which cut across the administrative map. Sources: Lonely Planet’s points-of-interest index, the UNESCO World Heritage List and English Wikipedia. Founding dates come from Wikidata and from the opening paragraph of each Wikipedia article; rows with no article of their own are dated by site type, which is an estimate rather than a source. Where the eras overlap — the Commonwealth opens in 1569 while the Jagiellonians run to 1572, and the partitions run from 1772 to 1795 — the table cuts at 1569 and 1795. Map links are searches, not pins: Lonely Planet publishes no coordinates.
