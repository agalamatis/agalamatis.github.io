All 12 of Austria’s World Heritage properties are tagged — 10 matched to existing Lonely Planet rows, 2 added as rows of their own — and both tentative-list entries are represented, marked WHS-T. Austria has never had a property delisted, so there is no former-site category. The Historic Centre of Vienna covers the Innere Stadt, the Ringstrasse and the Belvedere rather than the whole city, so it is tagged attraction by attraction, with Schönbrunn as a separate property; Salzburg, Graz, the Wachau and Hallstatt-Dachstein are tagged at destination level. Rows are assigned to states by town, not by Lonely Planet’s own regional pages, which cut across the state map. Sources: Lonely Planet’s points-of-interest index, the UNESCO World Heritage List and English Wikipedia — 51 German-title matches that turned out to be generic encyclopedia articles or foreign namesakes were dropped after checking each against its own opening paragraph. Founding dates come from Wikidata and from the opening paragraph of each Wikipedia article; rows with no article of their own are dated by site type, which is an estimate rather than a source. The interregnum of 1246–1278 is counted with Babenberg, and the table cuts at 1780 as specified, so the Theresian decades sit in Division of Lands.
